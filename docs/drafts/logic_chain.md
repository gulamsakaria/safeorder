*For Facebook-commerce buyers and small sellers in Bangladesh, advance payments to unverifiable pages
cause irreversible losses and slow, argument-based dispute handling. We built SafeOrder, an AI-assisted
trust and dispute system that uses synthetic seller behaviour and dispute evidence to score seller
risk before payment and to recommend explainable dispute outcomes to a human analyst. Success is
measured by recall of high-risk sellers at a fixed false-alarm rate, wrong-refund and wrong-rejection
rates, and analyst time per dispute.*

| Step | Our answer |
|---|---|
| 1. User | A buyer paying a Facebook seller; a small honest seller; an upay dispute analyst. |
| 2. Problem | Advance-payment fraud and unfair, slow disputes. Baseline to measure: share of fake-seller orders and handling minutes per dispute (*not measured yet*). |
| 3. Why now | Wallet behaviour is rich, dispute text is Bangla and informal, and small models run privately and cheaply. |
| 4. Solution | Trust Check, a held Safe Order, two-sided dispute report, evidence analyzer, analyst console, feedback loop. |
| 5. AI role | Prediction (seller risk), classification (dispute type), detection (inconsistency, injection), explanation (reasons, timeline). |
| 6. Impact | Fewer buyer losses, faster disputes, a track record for honest sellers. *Not measured*; a pilot would measure it. |
| 7. Data | Synthetic seller tables; dispute cases written by an AI assistant (not yet by the team); no real data. |
| 8. Validation | Held-out generator (v2), baselines, fairness and injection tests. The dispute cases have one author, so a team-written test set is still needed. |
| 9. Scale | Validation on governed, anonymised data; integration through order and ledger events; monitoring and review service levels. |
